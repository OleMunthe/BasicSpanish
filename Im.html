THEORY

In Spanish, feelings, states, and conditions are expressed using different verbs. The most common ones are estar, ser, and tener. Choosing the correct verb is essential, as the meaning can change completely.

English Spanish
to be (state)estar
I am (state)estoy
to be (characteristic)ser
I am (characteristic)soy
to havetener
I havetengo
Quick tip:
🎭 estar = how you feel / where you are
🧬 ser = who you are / what defines you
🍽️ tener = hunger, thirst, temperature

Examples:

English Spanish
I am happy
(I'm happy at the moment)		estoy feliz
I am happy
(I'm a happy person)		soy feliz
I am boring
(I feel bored right now)		estoy aburrido
I am boring
(I'm a boring person)		soy aburrido
I am hungry
(I have hunger)		tengo hambre

1. Using estar for temporary states

The verb estar is used for temporary states, emotions, physical conditions, and locations.

English Spanish
I am tired estoy cansado
I am happy estoy feliz
I am sad estoy triste
I am sick estoy enfermo
I am angry estoy enfadado
I am bored estoy aburrido
I am worried estoy preocupado
I am at home estoy en casa
I am on vacation estoy de vacaciones

2. Using ser for permanent characteristics

The verb ser is used for permanent or defining characteristics, such as identity, personality, or inherent qualities.

English Spanish
I am rich soy rico
I am Spanish soy español
I am a student soy estudiante

3. Using tener for physical needs and sensations

In Spanish, physical needs and sensations are often expressed using the verb tener, not estar.

English Spanish
I am hungry tengo hambre
I am thirsty tengo sed
I am cold (temperature) tengo frío
I am hot (temperature) tengo calor

Learning which verb to use will help you sound more natural and avoid common mistakes when describing how you feel.
