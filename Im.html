THEORY

In Spanish, feelings, states, and conditions are expressed using different verbs. The most common ones are estar, ser, and tener. Choosing the correct verb is essential, as the meaning can change completely.

1. Using estar for temporary states

The verb estar is used for temporary states, emotions, physical conditions, and locations.

Examples: estoy cansado (I am tired), estoy feliz (I am happy), estoy enfermo (I am sick), estoy en casa (I am at home)

2. Using ser for permanent characteristics

The verb ser is used for permanent or defining characteristics, such as identity, personality, or inherent qualities.

Example: soy rico (I am rich)

3. Using tener for physical needs

In Spanish, physical needs such as hunger and thirst are expressed using the verb tener, not estar.

Examples: tengo hambre (I am hungry), tengo sed (I am thirsty)

Learning which verb to use will help you sound more natural and avoid common mistakes when describing how you feel.
