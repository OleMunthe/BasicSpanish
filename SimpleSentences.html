THEORY

Many everyday meanings in Spanish are expressed through short, fixed expressions. These should often be learned as whole units rather than translated word by word.

1. Fixed expressions (phrases to memorize)

The following expressions are some of the most common, and are best learned by heart. They are complete answers or reactions and are often used on their own.

English Spanish
See you nos vemos
You're welcome de nada
Of course por supuesto
That sounds good suena bien
That's all eso es todo
Good morning buenos días
Good night buena noche
Excuse me perdón
I don't know no sé
I understand entiendo
Nice to meet you mucho gusto
How are you? ¿cómo estás?
I'm fine estoy bien
Please por favor
Thank you very much muchas gracias

2 .Direct and indirect object pronouns: lo, la, le

Spanish uses object pronouns to avoid repeating nouns. These pronouns are placed before the verb in simple sentences.

  • lo = him / it (masculine, direct object)
  • la = her / it (feminine, direct object)
  • le = to him / to her (indirect object)
Meaning Spanish
I spoke to him/her le hablé
I kissed him lo besé
I kissed her la besé
I invited him lo invité
I invited her la invité

👉 Tip: Ask yourself what? or who? → lo / la
Ask to whom? → le

3. Using hace (weather, time, and actions)

The verb hacer is used in several common situations:

  • Weather: It is cold, hot, windy, etc.
  • Time: How long ago something happened
  • General actions: do / make
Meaning Spanish
It is cold hace frío
It is nice weather hace buen tiempo
Two days ago hace dos días
He/she does it lo hace
He/she makes coffee hace café

👉 Note: Spanish uses hace where English often use is, has been, or ago.
