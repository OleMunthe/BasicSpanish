THEORY

Questions are essential when you want to ask for information, directions, help, prices, or simply keep a conversation going. You will practice common question words such as qué (what), cómo (how), cuánto (how much), dónde (where), and por qué (why), as well as questions that do not use a question word.

Commands are used when you want to tell or ask someone to do something, for example Listen!, Wait!, Help me!, or Don't do it!. In Spanish, positive and negative commands are formed differently, and you will also learn where to place pronouns such as me, lo, and la.

The examples below mainly use the informal tú form, which is used when speaking to a friend, family member, child, or someone else you address informally.

1. Questions

Some of the most common Spanish question words are:

English Spanish
What? ¿Qué?
Why? ¿Por qué?
How? ¿Cómo?
How much? ¿Cuánto?
Where? ¿Dónde?

The list below contains some common Spanish questions that use these words and are useful in everyday conversations.

The translations are not always direct, word-for-word translations. For example, ¿Cómo te llamas? means What's your name?, but a more literal translation would be How do you call yourself?.

English Spanish
What's happening? ¿Qué pasa?
What does it mean? ¿Qué significa?
What is that? ¿Qué es eso?
What time is it? ¿Qué hora es?
What do you want to do? ¿Qué quieres hacer?
Why not? ¿Por qué no?
What's your name? ¿Cómo te llamas?
What's your name? (formal) ¿Cómo se llama?
How are you? ¿Cómo estás?
How much does it cost? ¿Cuánto cuesta?
How do you say - in Spanish? ¿Cómo se dice - en español?
How can I help you? ¿Cómo puedo ayudarte?
Where is the beach? ¿Dónde está la playa?
Where is the toilet/bathroom? ¿Dónde está el baño?

There are, of course, also many questions that do not use a question word. Here are some common examples:

English Spanish
Can you help me? ¿Puedes ayudarme?
Can you repeat it? ¿Puedes repetirlo?
Can we come in? ¿Podemos entrar?
Do you want it? ¿Lo quieres?
Is it far? ¿Está lejos?
Does it hurt? ¿Te duele?
Is there a shop nearby? ¿Hay una tienda cerca de aquí?
Is there a beach nearby? ¿Hay una playa cerca de aquí?

2. Positive commands 😊

Positive commands tell someone to do something. The examples in this section use the informal tú("you") form.

For most verbs, the positive tú command has the same form as the third-person singular form (él/ella/usted) of the present tense.

For example:
hablas = you speak ❌ (statement)
habla = speak ✅ (command)

More examples:

English Spanish
Listen! ¡Escucha!
Repeat! ¡Repite!
Come in. Pasa.
Wait! ¡Espera!
Run! ¡Corre!

Some common positive tú commands are irregular and must be learned separately:

  • hace (he makes) → haz (do/make)
  • dice (he says) → di (say),
  • va (he goes) → ve (go)
  • pone (he puts) → pon (put),
  • sale (he leaves) → sal (leave)
  • viene (he comes) → ven (come).
  • tiene (he has) → ten (have).

3. Pronouns with commands (-me, -lo, -la)

With positive commands, object pronouns are attached to the end of the verb.

  • repite → repítelo (repeat it)
  • prueba → pruébalo (try it)
  • escucha → escúchame (listen to me)
  • ayuda → ayúdame (help me)
  • da → dame (give me)
  • da → dámelo (give it to me)

👉 When pronouns are added, the verb often gets an accent mark to keep the original stress.

4. Negative commands 😠

Negative tú commands are formed differently from positive commands. The pronoun comes before the verb.

As a general rule:

  • -ar verbs take an -es ending: hablar → no hables
  • -er verbs take an -as ending: comer → no comas
  • -ir verbs take an -as ending: escribir → no escribas

If there is an object pronoun, the usual pattern is: no + pronoun + verb.

Compare:

  • Ayúdame. – Help me!
  • No me ayudes. – Don't help me!

More examples:

English Spanish
Don't give it to me! ¡No me lo des!
Give it to me! ¡Dámelo!
Don't try it! ¡No lo pruebes!
Try it! ¡Pruébalo!
Don't repeat it! ¡No lo repitas!
Repeat it! ¡Repítelo!
Don't listen! ¡No escuches!
Listen! ¡Escucha!
Don't listen to me! ¡No me escuches!
Listen to me! ¡Escúchame!
Don't throw it! ¡No lo tires!
Throw it! ¡Tíralo!
