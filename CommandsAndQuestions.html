THEORY

In this quiz you practice questions and commands. Many of them follow clear patterns — especially imperatives and object pronouns — while others should simply be memorized.

1. Questions

The spanish "question words" are the following:

English Spanish
What? ¿Qué?
Why? ¿Por qué?
How? ¿Cómo?
How much? ¿Cuánto?
Where? ¿Dónde?

The list below contains some common Spanish questions with these words that are useful in everyday conversations.

The translations are not always direct, word-for-word translations. For example, ¿Cómo te llamas? means What's your name?, but a more literal translation would be How do you call yourself?

English Spanish
What's happening? ¿Qué pasa?
What does it mean? ¿Qué significa?
What is that? ¿Qué es eso?
What time is it? ¿Qué hora es?
What do you want to do? ¿Qué quieres hacer?
Why not? ¿Por qué no?
What's your name? ¿Cómo te llamas?
What's your name? (formal) ¿Cómo se llama?
How are you? ¿Cómo estás?
How much does it cost? ¿Cuánto cuesta?
How do you say – in Spanish? ¿Cómo se dice – en español?
How can I help you? ¿Cómo puedo ayudarte?
Where is the beach? ¿Dónde está la playa?
Where is the toilet/bathroom? ¿Dónde está baño?

There are (of course) questions whitout the "questions words", for example the following:

English Spanish
Can you help me? ¿Puedes ayudarme?
Can you repeat it? ¿Puedes repetirlo?
Can we come in? ¿Podemos entrar?
Do you want it? ¿Lo quieres?
Is it far? ¿Está lejos?
Does it hurt? ¿Te duele?
Is there a shop nearby? ¿Hay una tienda cerca de aquí?

2. Positive commands (imperative)

Positive commands tell someone to do something. Even though the command is directed at you (tú), Spanish does not use the normal -s ending. Instead, positive commands use the third person singular form (él/ella).

For example:
hablas = you speak ❌ (statement)
habla = speak! ✅ (command)

More examples:

English Spanish
listen escucha
repeat repite
come in pasa
listen escucha
wait espera
run corre

3. Adding pronouns to the end of commands (‑me, ‑lo, ‑la)

With positive commands, object pronouns are attached to the end of the verb.

  • repite → repítelo (Repeat it)
  • prueba → pruébalo (Try it)
  • escucha → escuchame (Listen to me)
  • ayuda → ayúdame (Help me)
  • da → dame (Give me)
  • da → dámelo (Give it to me)

👉 When pronouns are added, the verb often gets an accent mark to keep the original stress.

4. Negative commands

Negative commands always use:

  • no + pronoun + verb
  • -es-ending instead of -as
  • -as-ending instead of -es

Compare the following:

  • Ayúdame – Help me!
  • No me ayudes – Don’t help me!

More examples:

English Spanish
Don’t give it to me No me lo des
Give it to me Damelo
Don’t try it No lo pruebes
Try it Pruebalo
Don’t repeat it No lo repitas
Repeat it Repitalo
Don’t listen No eschuches
Listen Eschucha
Don’t listen to me No me eschuches
Listen Eschuchame
Don't throw it No lo tires
Throw it tíralo
