THEORY

Unlike regular verbs, irregular verbs in Spanish do not follow a single, predictable pattern in the present tense. Their verb stems or endings may change, and these changes must be learned individually.

Many of the most common Spanish verbs are irregular. Learning how they are conjugated is essential for understanding and using Spanish in everyday situations.

1. Irregular verbs with "-go" in the “yo” form

Some verbs takes an "-go" in the first person singular yo-form:

tener (to have) → tengo (I have)

The other forms often follow regular patterns, or more or less regular patternes; Sometimes "e" changes to "ie":

tener (to have) → tiene (he/she has)

Verb yo tú él / ella nosotros ellos
hacer (to do / make) hago haces hace hacemos hacen
decir (to say / tell) digo dices dice decimos dicen
salir (to leave / go out) salgo sales sale salimos salen
tener (to have) tengo tienes tiene tenemos tienen
venir (to come) vengo vienes viene venimos vienen

2. The verb "saber" (to know)

The verb saber (to know) is highly irregular in the yo-form, but regular in the other forms:

Verb yo tú él / ella nosotros ellos
saber (to know) sé sabes sabe sabemos saben

3. The verb "ir" (to go)

The verb ir (to go) is highly irregular and must be memorized.

Verb yo tú él / ella nosotros ellos
ir (to go) voy vas va vamos van

4. The verbs “ser” and “estar” (to be)

Spanish has two verbs that mean “to be”: ser and estar. Both are irregular.

One uses ser for characteristics and estar for states:

🎭 estar = how you feel / where you are
🧬 ser = who you are / what defines you

Meaning yo tú él / ella nosotros ellos
ser soy eres es somos son
estar (state) estoy estás está estamos están
